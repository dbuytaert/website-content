# Whitehouse.gov using Drupal

Big, exciting news! The flagship website of the U.S. government, [WhiteHouse.gov](http://whitehouse.gov), just relaunched on Drupal. This is a big day for [Drupal](https://www.drupal.org) and Open Source in government, and it is something all of us in the community should be very proud of.

![Screenshot of the White House website featuring a weekly address by the President and information on health insurance reform.](http://default/files/cache/drupal/whitehouse-gov-640w.jpg)

First of all, I think Drupal is a perfect match for President Barack Obama's push for an open and transparent government. Drupal provides a great mix of traditional web content management features and social features that enable open communication and participation. This combination is what we refer to as *social publishing* and is why so many people use Drupal.

Furthermore, I think Drupal is a great fit in terms of President Barack Obama's desire to reduce costs and act quickly. Drupal's flexibility and modularity enable organizations to build sites quickly and at a lower cost than most other systems. In other words, Drupal is a great match for the U.S. government.

Second, this is a clear sign that governments realize Open Source does not pose additional risks compared with proprietary software. By moving away from proprietary software, they avoid being locked into a particular technology and can benefit from the innovation that results from thousands of developers collaborating on Drupal. It takes time to understand these things and bring about this change, so I congratulate the Obama administration for taking such an important leadership role in considering Open Source solutions.

As one of the world's largest consumers of computer software, the U.S. government is not new to Drupal. Several agencies, including the Department of Defense, the Department of Commerce, the Department of Education, and the General Services Administration, have been using Drupal, for example. Drupal adoption is growing rapidly within the U.S. government.

However, WhiteHouse.gov's switch to Drupal goes above and beyond any other Drupal installation within the U.S. government and is a fantastic testament to Drupal and Open Source. It will raise awareness of Drupal across the U.S. government and governments worldwide.

Personally, I'm thrilled by the idea that Drupal can help governments provide greater transparency, higher velocity, and more flexibility.
